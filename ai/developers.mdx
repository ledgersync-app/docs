<Accordion title="Who can connect: the details">
  - Firms that use the LedgerSync app: the owner, administrators and employees each sign in with their own
    LedgerSync app login (email or username, password, and the two-step code if the login has one). Each sees
    only what they see in the app: an employee sees the clients assigned to them.
  - Firms new to LedgerSync sign up in the LedgerSync app first
    ([ledgersyncappv2.com/sign-up](https://ledgersyncappv2.com/sign-up)), then connect like any other firm. Until
    our team has checked a new firm, `ledgersync_create_connect_link` answers `firm_under_review` and
    `ledgersync_get_account_status` adds the same sentence; every other tool works.
  - Approved developer accounts: type the developer portal email on the LedgerSync sign-in page. It sends you
    to the developer portal sign-in. The account must be approved for that environment; live also needs the
    payment step. An application still waiting for its first approval cannot connect yet.
  - Sub-account (firm) logins and their employees cannot connect yet; they get **Connecting an AI assistant is
    not available for sub-account logins yet.** Client logins, support sessions and logins with access days and
    hours cannot connect.
  - ChatGPT needs a paid plan (Plus or higher) and the web version on chatgpt.com. If **Create MCP App** is
    missing, turn on **Developer mode** (**Settings**, then **Security and login**). On ChatGPT Business,
    Enterprise or Edu, only the workspace admin can add it for everyone.
  - On Claude Team and Enterprise, an Owner first adds it under **Organization settings > Connectors** (**Add**,
    then **Custom**, then **Web**). Members then open **Connectors**, find LedgerSync and click **Connect**. The
    Claude Free plan allows one custom connector. Once added, it also works in Claude Desktop, the Claude
    mobile apps, and Claude Code signed in with the same account.
  - Grok works on any Grok plan, even the free one, on grok.com in a web browser: open
    [grok.com/connectors](https://grok.com/connectors), click **New Connector**, then **Custom**, and enter the
    address. On Grok Business and Enterprise, xAI's docs say a team admin first adds it in the xAI console
    ([console.x.ai](https://console.x.ai): **Grok Business**, then **Connectors**); each member then connects
    their own account at grok.com/connectors. In our check, Grok called a tool that changes data
    (`ledgersync_create_client`) without asking first. Claude and ChatGPT ask before they change something.
  - Grok Bot, Grok's agent, adds LedgerSync when you ask it to: "Add a custom remote MCP server named LedgerSync
    with URL https://api.ledgersyncappv2.com/mcp". It signs in with Cursor's client id
    (`https://cursor.com/oauth/mcp-client.json`), so the sign-in asks you to allow **Cursor**. Every app that
    signs in with that id, Cursor's own included, is approved too. See [Set up in Grok Bot](/ai/setup-grok-bot).
  - Other AI apps are not supported yet (they are blocked): only Claude, ChatGPT, Grok and apps that sign in
    with Cursor's client id are approved. Ask support if you need one.
</Accordion>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Tool details: refreshes left, which firm and login, the firm's own accounts, duplicates, client messages and invitations, the desktop app, statement conversions, QuickBooks bookkeeping, rules setup">
    - `ledgersync_get_account_status` also says whether the connection is full or read-only. Refreshes left
      is your firm's shared daily budget, not the per-app cap.
    - When `access.status` is `active`, `ledgersync_get_account_status` also names the firm and the login the
      connection uses, in `account`: `firm_name`, `signed_in_as` (the login's email) and `role` (`owner`, `admin` or `employee`).
      A name LedgerSync could not read is left out.
    - `ledgersync_find_clients` also returns the firm's own accounts: the banks connected on the firm owner's
      own LedgerSync login rather than for a client, as the LedgerSync app's client list shows them. They come
      back as one entry, first in the list, whose name ends in "(the firm's own accounts)", marked
      `firm_own: true` and with no email. Only the owner and administrators get it, never employees, and only
      when the owner's login holds a bank connection and the search is blank or matches the firm's name or the
      owner's name, username or email.
    - The firm's own entry's id works with the other client tools, except client messages: LedgerSync never
      messages the firm itself, so a reconnect preview leaves out the firm's own connections and an invitation
      cannot go to it.
    - `ledgersync_create_client` checks for an existing client with the same name or email first. A client
      added through the AI is like a client added in the app: they can sign in to LedgerSync, and a bank they
      connect brings your firm's usual number of past statements.
    - `ledgersync_create_connect_link` creates a secure page where the account holder picks their bank and
      what to connect (transactions, statements, check images), then signs in. It is the LedgerSync app's own
      bank picker: the same bank list as the app's **Add Bank Connection** screen, and the same choice of
      connections. `ledgersync_get_fix_link` creates one where they sign in to their bank again to fix a broken
      connection. On both, the sign-in happens on LedgerSync's page or on the bank-data partner's secure page
      it opens, depending on the bank.
    - When what they pick needs more than one connection, for example Regions Bank statements and checks, the
      page goes through them one at a time, and each sign-in opens in a new tab.
    - To email or text a client, the AI is pointed to `ledgersync_preview_client_message` and
      `ledgersync_send_client_message`, never to your own email app: LedgerSync sends the message itself,
      with its own link for that client.
    - `ledgersync_create_connect_link` with `for_check_images: true` makes the same link with Checks already
      ticked. Check images come only through LedgerSync's own connection, so for many banks they need one
      sign-in for it and another for transactions or statements. That connection is created as soon as the
      account holder confirms it on the page; if they stop before signing in, use `ledgersync_get_fix_link` on
      that connection instead of a new link. The images show in the LedgerSync app; no tool returns them.
    - `ledgersync_search_institutions` also says whether a bank gives transactions, statements and check
      images. When a bank in the answer gives check images, the result adds a `check_images_note` on how to
      connect them. `ledgersync_list_connections` also gives each connection's status.
    - `ledgersync_list_connections_needing_attention` lists the connections that are not working, across all
      your clients, the ones that need the account holder first. For the owner and administrators it also lists
      the ones on the firm's own accounts, marked `firm_own: true`, with the firm's own entry's id as
      `client_id`.
    - `ledgersync_preview_client_message` comes first. It finds the clients whose bank connection needs
      attention: all of them, the ones at a bank you name (for example American Express), or the connections
      you pick. It says how many messages will go out, who is skipped and why, and which link each client
      gets, and shows a sample. It sends nothing and creates no link.
    - Each message carries that client's own link where the text says `#LS_URL#`: a link to fix the
      connection or, for a bank LedgerSync now connects through MX (such as American Express), a link to
      connect that bank again through MX. A client with several broken connections at such a bank gets one
      message. At any other bank, a client gets one message for each connection that needs attention, each
      with that connection's own link. When some clients get a fix link and others an MX link, the preview
      says so, so you can word the message to fit both.
    - Messages can also use `#CLIENT_NAME#`, `#BANK_NAME#`, `#COMPANY_NAME#` (your firm's name) and
      `#EMPLOYEE_FIRST_NAME#` (the first name of the person sending). An email without a subject gets one
      like "Acme CPA needs you to reconnect your bank", with your firm's name.
    - `ledgersync_preview_client_message` with `purpose: "invite"` prepares an invitation instead, for the
      clients you name in `client_ids` (up to 200) rather than for connections or a bank. Any client you see
      can be invited, even one with banks already connected: the preview shows how many connections each one
      has. Like a reconnect preview, it sends nothing and creates no link.
    - Each invitation carries that client's own sign-in link where the text says `#LS_URL#`. It signs the
      client in to their LedgerSync account with nothing to type, and they connect their bank there. The link
      works for 6 days.
    - An invitation can use `#CLIENT_NAME#`, `#COMPANY_NAME#`, `#EMPLOYEE_FIRST_NAME#` and
      `#ACCOUNTANT_NAME#` (the full name of the person sending), but not `#BANK_NAME#`: an invitation is not
      about one bank. An invitation email without a subject gets one like "Acme CPA invites you to connect
      your bank". Invitations count toward the same daily limit as other client messages.
    - `ledgersync_send_client_message` sends only what a preview showed, using the preview's confirmation.
      Use it within 30 minutes; once sending starts, it keeps working for 24 hours. An expired one is refused
      with `invalid_request`: preview again.
    - Each send call works for about 25 seconds and returns running totals. The AI calls again with the same
      confirmation until no client is left. A client never gets the same message twice from one
      confirmation, even when a call is repeated.
    - Emails go to every email address on file for the client. Texts go to the client's main phone number.
    - Each client comes back sent, sent with an MX link, skipped or failed (with a reason), or unknown:
      LedgerSync could not confirm it went out, so it is not sent again. A client a call had no time for is
      sent on the next call. On sandbox, every client that would get the message comes back as would send.
    - A client is skipped when:
      - the bank has no working way to reconnect right now, so there is no link to send;
      - the client got a LedgerSync message about that connection in the last 24 hours, unless you asked to
        include them;
      - the AI connector cannot reach the client's record;
      - for an email, the client has no email address (a client added through the AI without an email has
        none);
      - for a text, texts are turned off for the client, there is no phone number (the AI adds clients
        without one), the client replied STOP (texts start again when they reply START), it is outside the
        client's texting hours, or LedgerSync's assistant is texting with the client right now;
      - for an invitation, the client was already invited through the AI in the last 24 hours (unless you
        asked to include them), is not one you can see in the LedgerSync app, or signs in through your firm's
        own integration instead of the LedgerSync app.
    - A client fails when something changed since the preview (the client was removed, the connection no
      longer needs attention, or it is no longer one you can see), when the client's link could not be
      created, or when the email or text service refused the message or could not confirm a text went out.
    - `ledgersync_list_message_templates` lists your firm's Issue templates (for reconnect messages) and
      Invitation templates (for invitations), email and text, and flags (`breaks_rules`) any that uses a
      placeholder its kind of message does not allow. `ledgersync_save_message_template` saves a new one, an
      Invitation template with `purpose: "invite"`, and never changes an existing one. They are the same
      templates as in the Communication Center of the LedgerSync app, shared with your whole firm. A preview
      can start from a saved template of its kind (an invitation from an Invitation template); editing the
      template later does not change that preview. Only the AI uses a template's email subject for now.
    - `ledgersync_get_desktop_app_status` checks the app live and returns the Windows and Mac download links
      and the setup steps.
    - `ledgersync_pair_desktop_app` replaces any computer linked before, so LedgerSync tells the AI to ask you to
      confirm first.
    - A bank or connection that needs the desktop app for refreshes carries `requires_desktop_app: true` in
      the search, connection and refresh-status results; the field is left out otherwise. A connection that is
      not working on such a bank also gets a `next_step` that says so.
    - `ledgersync_create_statement_upload_link` returns a secure LedgerSync page where the user uploads one bank
      statement PDF (up to 30 MB), and a `conversion_id` (`cnv_...`). The AI cannot pass a file to LedgerSync,
      even one attached to the chat. LedgerSync reads it with the same reader as the PDF Converter in the app
      and `POST /v3/statements/extract`; nothing is added to a client. See
      [Turn a bank statement PDF into transactions](/ai/convert-statement).
    - `ledgersync_get_statement_conversion` says `waiting_for_upload` (with `upload_problem` when an upload was
      refused, in words the AI can repeat), `in_progress`, `completed`, `failed`, `expired` or `deleted` (the
      person deleted it on the PDF Converter page in the app, so it has no transactions or file). A completed one
      gives the page count, the number of transactions, whether they match the statement's balances and why not
      (`validated`; `validation_status`: `balanced`, `balance_mismatch`, `totals_mismatch` or `balance_missing`;
      `validation_message`, in words the AI can repeat; a conversion with no transactions has none of these) and
      the transactions, 50 at a time by default (up to 200): pass `next_cursor` for more.
    - `ledgersync_get_conversion_download_link` makes a link to the file the LedgerSync app's PDF Converter
      makes: `csv`, `xlsx` (Excel), `qbo` (a QuickBooks bank file) or `ofx`.
    - A conversion belongs to the person who made the upload link: the owner, an administrator or an employee.
      It also shows on that person's **PDF Converter** page in the LedgerSync app, where it can be exported
      again.
    - QuickBooks bookkeeping is for clients whose bank transactions do not come into QuickBooks through a
      QuickBooks bank feed. The AI categorizes the client's transactions waiting in LedgerSync and, after the user
      approves a preview, LedgerSync posts them into the client's QuickBooks Online. See
      [Bookkeeping into QuickBooks](/ai/quickbooks-bookkeeping). Accounts on LedgerSync's own connection
      (`acc_FDE_...`) are not supported yet and are refused with `invalid_request`.
    - `ledgersync_get_quickbooks_setup` says whether QuickBooks is `connected`, `not_connected` or `broken` (with
      the reason) for a client. It lists the client's accounts with the QuickBooks bank account each posts to, and
      the client's active QuickBooks Bank and Credit Card accounts. Its `next_step` tells the AI to ask the user
      which QuickBooks account an account without one posts to, and to confirm those QuickBooks accounts have no
      QuickBooks bank feed.
    - `ledgersync_list_transactions_to_categorize` lists one account's transactions that are on the For review
      list, posted (not pending), not yet sent to QuickBooks and not excluded by a rule, 50 at a time by default
      (up to 100; pass `next_cursor` for more), between optional `from` and `to` dates. Each carries LedgerSync's
      suggestion when it has one (category, payee, the rule that made it), `possible_duplicate` when LedgerSync
      flagged it as possibly in QuickBooks already, and, on Finicity accounts, `possible_transfer`. A row one of the
      firm's global rules matched but could not file, because the client has no QuickBooks category mapped to the
      rule's global category, carries `pending_global_rule` (`rule_name`, `global_category`). The tool's
      `next_step` tells the AI to keep LedgerSync's suggestions and to ask the user about those rows.
    - `ledgersync_list_quickbooks_categories` lists the client's active QuickBooks categories from LedgerSync's
      copy of the client's chart of accounts, a page at a time, narrowed by `query`.
      `ledgersync_search_quickbooks_payees` finds active vendors (from LedgerSync's copy), customers and
      employees (read from QuickBooks), 50 by default and up to 200, by `query` and `type` (`vendor`, `customer`
      or `employee`), and says when more matched (`truncated`). A category or vendor added in QuickBooks shows
      after **Sync QuickBooks Data** in the LedgerSync app.
    - `ledgersync_preview_quickbooks_push` takes up to 100 `rows` (`transaction_id`, `category_id`, and
      `payee_id` with `payee_type` or a `new_payee_name`) and up to 5 `account_mappings` (`account_id`,
      `qbo_bank_account_id`). It checks every row and mapping and writes nothing. It returns the totals (money in,
      money out), the totals by category, the new payees, the QuickBooks accounts used, every refused row with
      its reason, a sample of up to 20 rows, a warning for each QuickBooks account used asking to confirm it has
      no bank feed, and, when at least one row can be posted, a `confirmation_id` and its `expires_at`.
    - A row is refused when it is not this client's, already categorized, sent or excluded, still pending,
      excluded by a rule, flagged as a possible duplicate, on an unknown or inactive category or payee, without a
      payee, a deposit with an employee as payee, or on an account with no QuickBooks bank account. A row whose
      categorization in LedgerSync already has a split, or a line with a class, its own amount or a billable
      customer, is refused as `has_split_or_class`, because the push would replace it with one line; accept it in
      the LedgerSync app. A vendor name longer than 80 characters (a new payee name, or an existing vendor's name in
      LedgerSync's copy) is refused as `payee_name_too_long`: use a shorter new name, or shorten the vendor's name
      in QuickBooks and click **Sync QuickBooks Data** in the LedgerSync app before previewing again. A new payee
      name that matches an existing payee uses that payee. A mapping is refused when the QuickBooks account is not
      an active Bank or Credit Card account, or another of the client's accounts already posts to it: each
      QuickBooks bank account goes with one account in LedgerSync.
    - `ledgersync_push_to_quickbooks` takes only the `confirmation_id` and posts exactly what the preview
      accepted. It saves the new account mappings, creates the new payees in QuickBooks as vendors, writes the
      category and payee on each transaction, moves it to Categorized, and queues the same upload to QuickBooks
      as the LedgerSync app's sync. As in the app, a new account mapping also goes on that account's other
      transactions, and a new payee on the client's other transactions not yet in QuickBooks that carry that
      name as a suggested payee (no payee id). A transaction that changed since the preview is refused. It
      returns a `push_id` (`qbp_...`), a status (`queued`, `nothing_to_push` or `already_queued`), the number
      queued, the refused transactions and the payees it created. Calling it again with the same confirmation
      returns the same answer and never posts twice.
    - `ledgersync_get_quickbooks_push_status` takes the `client_id` and the `push_id` and says `in_progress`,
      `finished` or `failed`, with counts and, for each transaction, `synced` (with its QuickBooks id), `failed`
      (with the reason), `skipped_possible_duplicate`, `skipped_not_posted`, `pending` or `not_pushed`, a page at
      a time.
    - The instructions on [Bookkeeping into QuickBooks](/ai/quickbooks-bookkeeping) are also an MCP prompt,
      `quickbooks_bookkeeping` ("Bookkeeping into QuickBooks"), with optional `client` and `period` arguments.
      Claude Code lists it as a command.
    - Rules setup lets the AI create a client's categorization rules in LedgerSync, with the new categories,
      category links and vendors they need, after the user approves a preview. It works for Manual clients now.
      For any other client, `ledgersync_get_rules_setup` answers `supported: false` with the reason in its
      `next_step`, and a preview is refused with `invalid_request`; for a QuickBooks client, the `next_step` points
      to the QuickBooks bookkeeping tools. See
      [Set up rules for a client](/ai/rules-setup). An AI app connected before these tools came out must load the
      new tool list first: reconnect in Claude, click **Refresh** in ChatGPT or reauthenticate in Grok, then start a
      new chat (see Tool updates below).
    - `ledgersync_get_rules_setup` takes the `client_id` and returns the client's `platform`, whether rules setup is
      `supported` for it, `counts` (the client's and the firm's categories and how many have a GL code, their
      vendors and rules, and the client's waiting transactions: all of them, the ones no rule holds, and the ones
      an existing rule matches), `can_create_global_rules`, the `limits` and the client's last setups (`setup_id`,
      `status`, `created_at`). Its `next_step` gives the AI the next steps, or says why the client is not
      supported.
    - `ledgersync_list_setup_categories` lists the client's categories (`kind: "client"`, ids `cat_c_...`, with
      their `gl_code` and the global category each is linked to, `firm_category`) or the firm's global categories
      (`kind: "firm"`, ids `cat_f_...`, with their `gl_code`, the client's category linked to it, `mapped_here`, and
      how many clients link it, `clients_mapped`), narrowed by `query` (a name or GL code), 50 at a time by default
      (up to 100).
    - `ledgersync_list_setup_vendors` lists the client's vendors (`kind: "client"`, ids `ven_c_...`) or the firm's
      global vendors (`kind: "firm"`, ids `ven_f_...`, with their `aliases`), narrowed by `query`, a page at a
      time.
    - `ledgersync_list_rules` lists the client's rules (`scope: "client"`, ids `rul_c_...`) or the firm's global
      rules (`scope: "global"`, ids `rul_g_...`), narrowed by `query`, a page at a time. Each rule has `enabled`,
      `priority`, `direction` (`money_in`, `money_out` or `both`), `match` (`all` or `any`), `rule_type`,
      `conditions` (`field`, `operator`, `value`), `category` (`id`, `name`, `gl_code`), `payee` (`id`, `name`)
      and `ai_generated`.
    - Each category, vendor and rule that a rules setup created or linked carries `ai` (`setup_id`, `reviewed`):
      the LS-AI mark.
    - `ledgersync_list_uncategorized_transactions` lists the client's waiting transactions that no rule holds and
      nobody categorized (not pending and not excluded), grouped by bank text, largest groups first, 50 groups at a
      time by default (up to 100), between optional `from` and `to` dates, and only money in or money out when
      `direction` is given. Each group has the bank text (`description`), `count`, `money_in`, `money_out`,
      `total`, `first_date`, `last_date`, `sources`, up to 3 `examples` and, when an existing rule would already
      fill it, `matched_by_rule`. `total_groups` and `total_rows` count everything.
    - `ledgersync_preview_rules_setup` takes the `client_id` and up to 100 `rules`, 50 `firm_categories`, 50
      `client_categories`, 100 `mappings` and 100 `vendors`. The AI gives each new rule, category and vendor a
      `ref` (lowercase letters, digits, `_` or `-`, up to 32 characters, unique in the preview). An item points at
      an existing object with `{"id": "..."}`, or at a new one in the same preview with `{"ref": "..."}`. A rule
      has a `scope` (client or global), a `name`, a `direction`, a `match` (all or any), 1 to 5 `conditions` on
      the bank text (an `operator` and a `value`), a `category` and an optional `payee`: a client category and
      vendor for a client rule, a global category and vendor for a global rule. A mapping links a firm (global)
      category to a client category. A vendor has a `scope` too.
    - The preview writes nothing. For each rule it returns whether it is OK or refused, how many waiting
      transactions it fills for this client (`fills_here`, with `examples`) and at the firm's other Manual clients
      (`fills_other_clients`, at `other_clients` clients), and its `warnings` (`code`, `message`). It also returns
      every refused item with its code and reason (and, for a name that is taken, `existing_id`: the id of the
      category, vendor or rule that has it), the totals and, when at least one item is OK, a `confirmation_id`.
    - A rule's warnings say when it fills nothing here or elsewhere, when some of its transactions go to a stronger
      rule it names (an existing rule, an earlier proposed rule or, for a global rule, any proposed client rule),
      when it shares transactions with other proposed rules, when its words
      are found inside longer words, when it fills more than half of the client's waiting transactions or more
      than 200 at other clients, when its category has no GL code, and, for a global rule, when this client has no
      category linked to the global category (the global category's GL code is then used).
    - An item is refused when: it is global and the login lacks the **Manage Global Rules** permission; a `ref` is
      repeated or unknown; an `id` is unknown or not the client's or the firm's; a name is empty, too long, already
      taken (a category or vendor whose name is taken comes back with `existing_id`, the id of the one that already
      has it, so the AI can use it instead; a rule whose name is taken needs another name) or twice in the preview;
      a global category is named like a GL code the firm already uses; a GL code is too long; a rule uses the wrong
      kind of category or vendor; a link clashes with an existing one; a condition is invalid, or none of them looks
      for words; LedgerSync's rule checks block the rule (the message says why); or it depends on a refused item.
      Refused rules are left out of the counts.
    - The preview's answer fits in 20,000 characters. When it would not, the preview leaves out the examples
      first, then the warning messages (the codes stay), then the refused items' messages (the codes stay), then
      some of the rules and refused items (counted in `more_rules` and `more_refused`), and says what it left out.
      The summary and the totals always cover everything.
    - `ledgersync_apply_rules_setup` takes only the `confirmation_id` and saves what the preview accepted, in the
      background. It returns a `setup_id` (`rsu_...`) and the status. LedgerSync first checks every item again: an
      item that is no longer valid fails with its reason, and a category or vendor whose name now exists is used
      instead of a new one. Then it fills the client's waiting transactions that no rule holds and nobody
      categorized, with the same rule order as for new transactions (the client's rules first, existing ones before
      new ones). When the setup made global rules, it also fills the waiting transactions at the firm's other
      Manual clients that one of those rules now wins. Calling it again with the same confirmation returns the
      first answer and never applies twice.
    - `ledgersync_get_rules_setup_status` takes the `client_id` and the `setup_id` and says where the setup is
      (queued, running, done, done with some failed items, failed, being undone, undone, or undo failed), with its
      phase and progress. A finished setup also gives what it created by type, how many transactions it filled for
      the client, how many the existing rules filled, how many it filled at other clients and at how many clients,
      and each failed item with its reason.
    - `ledgersync_undo_rules_setup` takes the `client_id`, the `setup_id` and an optional `undo_id`. Without an
      `undo_id`, it writes nothing: it says how many rules it would delete, how many waiting transactions it would
      clear and at how many clients, how many links it would put back, and which created items it would delete or
      keep (in use). It returns an `undo_id` that works for 15 minutes, only for the person and the AI app that
      asked. Called again with that `undo_id`, it undoes the setup: it deletes the setup's rules and clears only
      the For review transactions they hold, puts the links back, then deletes the created vendors and categories
      that nothing uses. Categorized transactions are never touched. A setup can be undone once it has stopped:
      done, done with some failed items, or failed (the undo then removes what it saved before it stopped), and
      again after a failed undo. While another setup of the firm is running or being undone, the call with the
      `undo_id` is refused with `invalid_request`: try it again when that one finishes.
    - A rules setup apply or undo that got no clear answer from LedgerSync comes back as `upstream_unknown`,
      naming the setup: check `ledgersync_get_rules_setup_status` before trying again.
    - The instructions on [Set up rules for a client](/ai/rules-setup) are also an MCP prompt, `rules_setup`
      ("Set up rules for a client"), with optional `client` and `focus` arguments. Claude Code lists it as a
      command.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Security: bank sign-ins never in the chat, 72-hour connect links, 10-minute one-use statement links, 6-day invitation sign-in links, client messages carry only LedgerSync links, QuickBooks posts only after a preview, rules saved only after a preview">
    - Bank sign-ins never happen in the chat. The account holder picks the bank on a LedgerSync page and signs
      in there, or on the bank-data partner's secure page it opens for some banks. Connect links expire after
      72 hours.
    - Statement download links expire after 10 minutes and work once; if a download fails, ask for a new link.
    - Statement upload links work for 24 hours and take one PDF. Converted-file links expire after 10 minutes
      and work once. Both stop working when the connection does: when it is disconnected or made read-only
      (uploads only), when the person's LedgerSync login can no longer connect, or when the connector is turned
      off.
    - Invitation links sign the client in to their LedgerSync account with nothing to type and expire
      after 6 days.
    - The connector cannot see or change API keys, webhooks or account settings.
    - Firms that sign in with the LedgerSync app login: changing the LedgerSync password, or turning off
      two-step sign-in, turns off every AI app connected with that login within a minute.
    - Developer accounts: open [portal.ledgersyncappv2.com](https://portal.ledgersyncappv2.com) on a computer,
      click **Connected apps**, then **Disconnect** next to each app. It cuts the app off at once; adding it
      again signs in fresh. The page lists only the apps of the environment picked in the portal's
      **Sandbox** / **Live** switch, so pick **Live** to see the live connector's apps. Anyone with the
      account's LedgerSync login can add it again. To turn it off for good, email support: support turns off
      the account's access for that environment, which stops every connected app and, on live, the account's
      API keys too.
    - The only link a client message can carry is that client's own LedgerSync link, which LedgerSync puts
      where the text says `#LS_URL#`. A message with any other web address is refused; email addresses are
      fine. A message that breaks a rule is refused with `invalid_request` and a sentence naming the problem.
      The same rules apply when saving a template.
    - An email shows your text as written, with the names and link filled in and your line breaks kept. HTML
      in the text shows as plain text.
    - Nothing is sent without a preview: LedgerSync tells the AI to show you who gets the message and a sample,
      and to ask you before it sends. Claude and ChatGPT do. In Grok, ask it to show you the message first: in
      our check, Grok tried to add a client without asking for approval. A preview's confirmation works only for
      the person and the AI app that made it.
    - Each person messages only the clients they see in the LedgerSync app: an employee, the clients assigned
      to them.
    - On the sandbox connector, client messages are never delivered and no link is created for them: the send
      shows what would be sent (would send).
    - Linking the desktop app needs the code the app shows, and LedgerSync tells the AI to ask you to confirm
      first. The code is never logged or repeated back.
    - The desktop app is linked to the firm owner's LedgerSync login. Only the owner can link it; a teammate's
      AI can check whether it is connected.
      Linking a computer replaces any computer linked before: only one computer is connected at a time.
      Every link is recorded and the LedgerSync team is alerted at once.
    - Nothing reaches QuickBooks without a preview. `ledgersync_push_to_quickbooks` takes only the preview's
      `confirmation_id`, which works for 30 minutes and only for the person and the AI app that made it, and posts
      only what the preview accepted. It is marked as changing data, so Claude and ChatGPT ask before it runs. In
      Grok, ask it to wait for your yes.
    - Each person posts to QuickBooks only for the clients they see in the LedgerSync app: an employee, the
      clients assigned to them.
    - Nothing is saved by a rules setup without a preview. `ledgersync_apply_rules_setup` takes only the preview's
      `confirmation_id`, which works for 30 minutes and only for the person and the AI app that made it, and saves
      only what the preview accepted. It and `ledgersync_undo_rules_setup` are marked as changing data, so Claude
      and ChatGPT ask before they run. In Grok, ask it to wait for your yes.
    - Each person sets up rules only for the clients they see in the LedgerSync app: an employee, the clients
      assigned to them. Global rules, global categories and global vendors also need the **Manage Global Rules**
      permission.

  <Accordion title="Refreshes and limits: 50 AI refreshes a day on live, 20 on sandbox, 300 client messages a day per firm on live, 60 QuickBooks pushes a day per AI app, 30 rules setups a day per AI app, per-minute and per-hour caps">
    - LedgerSync refreshes every working connection about once a day. A refresh you ask for takes a few
      minutes; see [Keeping data fresh](/guides/data-freshness).
    - A connection refreshed in the last 30 minutes is skipped unless you ask for a new pull anyway.
    - Each AI app can start 50 refreshes a day on live (20 on sandbox). They also count against your firm's
      daily budget of 500 on live (100 on sandbox), shared with API keys, which is the number the AI shows as
      refreshes left.
    - Per connected AI app: 50 new clients a day, 30 connect links an hour, 60 message previews an hour, 30
      saved templates a day, and 120 tool calls a minute on sandbox (60 on live).
    - Statement conversions: 3 at a time and 500 a day per firm on live (2 and 50 on sandbox), shared with
      the firm's API keys, in UTC days. Each AI app can make 30 upload links an hour. The upload page takes 5
      tries a minute per link, and a link stops after 3 failed uploads. Every conversion is a real OCR read, on
      sandbox too.
    - Long lists of connections, accounts, transactions and statements come back in pages.
    - Client messages: 300 a day per firm on live (100 on sandbox), counted across everyone at the firm and
      every AI app, in UTC days. Skipped and failed messages do not count. When the limit is reached, sending
      stops and the AI says so.
    - A client who got a LedgerSync message about the same connection in the last 24 hours is skipped, unless
      you ask the AI to include them.
    - A client invited through the AI in the last 24 hours is skipped, unless you ask the AI to include them.
    - Texts go out only between 11 am and 9 pm US Eastern time, unless the client has their own texting
      hours on file: then only within those hours, in their time zone, never before 8 am or after 9 pm. A
      client outside those hours is skipped; ask again later.
    - An email can be up to 5,000 characters and a text up to 600, with the names and link filled in. An
      email subject can be up to 150 characters.
    - A preview looks at up to 200 connections that need attention. When more match, it says so: name a bank
      or pick the connections to narrow it.
    - An invitation names up to 200 clients; a longer list is refused.
    - A refresh stopped by the desktop app (`desktop_app_required` or `desktop_app_route_choice`) never
      reached the bank. It counts against neither daily refresh limit, and the 30-minute skip does not block
      trying again.
    - Linking the desktop app: 10 tries an hour and 20 a day per LedgerSync account (UTC hours and days),
      whether the code works or not. A code that is not 7 letters or digits is refused without counting.
    - QuickBooks bookkeeping: one preview or push takes up to 100 transactions, 10 new payees and 5 new account
      mappings. Per connected AI app: 120 previews an hour and 60 pushes a day; a push counts only when it reaches
      LedgerSync. A preview's confirmation works for 30 minutes, and a push's answer is kept for 24 hours.
    - QuickBooks pushes: LedgerSync sends a few at a time, shared by every firm. A push past that is refused as
      busy (`rate_limit_exceeded`, "Another push is running; try again in a minute."), is not counted, and can be
      sent again a minute later with the same `confirmation_id`.
    - Rules setup: one preview takes up to 100 rules, 50 new global categories, 50 new client categories, 100
      category links and 100 new vendors (client and firm vendors together). Per connected AI app: 60 previews an
      hour, 30 applies a day and 10 undos a day; an apply counts only when it reaches LedgerSync. A preview's
      confirmation works for 30 minutes, an apply's answer is kept for 24 hours, and an undo check works for 15
      minutes.
    - Rules setup conditions and names: a rule has 1 to 5 conditions on the bank text, each 1 to 200 characters,
      at least one of them contains or is exactly; no amount conditions. Names: a client rule up to 100 characters, a global rule 64, a
      global category 100, a client category 64, a client vendor 80, a global vendor 64 and a GL code 64.
    - Rules setup applies: LedgerSync takes a few at a time, shared by every firm. An apply past that is refused as
      busy, is not counted, and can be sent again a minute later with the same `confirmation_id`. A firm runs one
      setup at a time; the next one waits until the one before it finishes. An undo does not wait: while another
      setup of the firm is running or being undone, it is refused with `invalid_request`, and can be sent again when
      that one finishes.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Error codes: 15 codes, what each means and what to do">
    Every error has a `code`, the same as the API's (see [Errors](/guides/errors)). Access errors (except
    `firm_under_review`, whose message is the whole sentence), desktop app errors and the per-minute limit
    also carry a `next_step` sentence the AI reads out. So does a connection
    or refresh stopped by the desktop app, in its result.

    The 15 codes you are most likely to see are listed below. The connector can also answer
    `invalid_request`, `internal_error`, `idempotency_conflict` or `upstream_unavailable` (LedgerSync could not
    be reached or could not check the firm just now; try again in a minute. After saving a template, list the
    templates first: it may already be saved). A QuickBooks push that got no clear answer from LedgerSync comes
    back as `upstream_unknown`, with its `push_id`: check `ledgersync_get_quickbooks_push_status` before pushing
    again. A rules setup apply or undo that got no clear answer comes back as `upstream_unknown` too, naming the
    setup: check `ledgersync_get_rules_setup_status` before trying again.

    | Code | What it means | What to do |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | `access_not_active` | This sign-in has no working LedgerSync account yet: it is not set up, the sign-up is not finished, it is being checked, we need an answer, it was not approved, it closed, or it was turned off. For a firm that signs in with its app login: the login cannot connect (a sub-account login), the person's login or the firm's account is locked, the firm owner turned off AI assistants for the person, the login has access days and hours, the person no longer has access to the firm, LedgerSync is not active for the firm, or connecting AI assistants is turned off for the firm. | Follow the step the AI reads out. Firms: [connect again](/ai/troubleshooting#connect-again) and sign in with the LedgerSync app login. Developers apply at [portal.ledgersyncappv2.com](https://portal.ledgersyncappv2.com). |
    | `access_request_not_approved` | This sign-in has a LedgerSync account, but not for this environment yet: its application is still being reviewed, needs more information, or has not finished the payment step. Or its access for this environment was turned off. | Follow the step the AI reads out: wait for the review, answer the question in the portal, or finish the payment step. If access was turned off, email support. |
    | `billing_not_configured` | Live access is approved, but no card is on file (the payment step was not completed). | Complete the payment step in the portal. |
    | `connector_app_pending_approval` | This AI app is not supported yet. Only Claude, ChatGPT and Grok are, Grok Bot included (it signs in with Cursor's client id, which is approved too). | Use Claude, ChatGPT or Grok, or email support. |
    | `connector_access_revoked` | This AI app was disconnected from your LedgerSync account, or the person who connected it changed their LedgerSync password or turned off two-step sign-in after it connected. | [Connect again](/ai/troubleshooting#connect-again) and sign in. |
    | `firm_under_review` | A new firm is still being reviewed by our team. It can add clients; bank connections open after the review. | Add clients now and connect banks after the review. |
    | `insufficient_scope` | This connection is read-only, or lacks permission for what you asked. | Ask for something the connection may do, or email support. |
    | `rate_limit_exceeded` | A limit was reached. | Try again when the AI says you can, or tomorrow for a daily limit. |
    | `not_found` | No client, connection, account, statement or conversion matches, or it belongs to another account. A conversion belongs to the person who made its upload link. | Ask the AI to find the client first, then try again. For a conversion, use the `conversion_id` from an upload link you made yourself. |
    | `desktop_app_required` | This bank needs the LedgerSync desktop app for refreshes, and the app is not connected right now. The bank was not called. | Open the desktop app on the computer it is set up on, then refresh again. The account holder has nothing to do. |
    | `desktop_app_route_choice` | The refresh is waiting for someone to choose whether LedgerSync reaches the bank through the desktop app. The bank was not called. | The AI gives you a fix link for the account holder. They make the choice on that LedgerSync page. Then refresh again. |
    | `pairing_code_invalid` | The desktop app code did not work: it is wrong, expired or already used. | Get a new code in the desktop app (**Settings**, **Connect with: AI chat**) and tell it to the AI. |
    | `desktop_app_session_closed` | The desktop app that showed the code is not open anymore. | Open the desktop app, get a new code and tell it to the AI. |
    | `desktop_service_unavailable` | LedgerSync could not reach the desktop app service. Nothing you did is wrong. | Try again in a few minutes. If it keeps failing, email support. |
    | `pairing_rate_limited` | Too many tries to link the desktop app. The AI says how many minutes to wait. | Wait that long, then try again with a new code. |
