## Start

<AiStep id="new-chat" n={1} total={5} title="Start a new chat">

In Claude, ChatGPT or Grok. In ChatGPT, also type **@** and pick **LedgerSync**.

</AiStep>

<AiStep id="ask" n={2} total={5} title="Ask the AI" img="/images/ai-1099/1099-1-ask.png" caption="Your request">

Type *"Which vendors need a 1099 for Acme?"*, with your client's name, and press **Enter**.

</AiStep>

<AiStep id="check" n={3} total={5} title="Check the list" img="/images/ai-1099/1099-2-list.png" caption="The list">

The AI lists the vendors paid enough to need a 1099, highest first. It says which ones are not marked for 1099 in
QuickBooks and which have no tax ID on file. It also says, from the expense accounts each vendor was paid through,
whether it looks like it needs a 1099, and why.

</AiStep>

<AiStep id="questions" n={4} total={5} title="Answer its questions">

When the accounts don't settle it, the AI asks you about the vendor, one at a time. For example: were the repairs
someone's work, or parts bought on their own? You decide.

</AiStep>

<AiStep id="preview" n={5} total={5} title="Say yes to the preview" imgs={[{ src: "/images/ai-1099/1099-3-preview.png", caption: "The preview" }, { src: "/images/ai-1099/1099-4-allow.png", caption: "Allow once circled" }]}>

Ask it to mark the vendors you checked. It shows each one before and after. Say yes, and allow it each time Claude or
ChatGPT asks. Nothing changes before that.

</AiStep>

<AiNote tone="green">

**Only some accounts count for 1099?** Tell the AI which ones, for example Contract Labor and Legal Fees. It counts
only those, as QuickBooks' own 1099 setup does. Nothing is saved, so tell it again next time.

</AiNote>

<AiNote tone="yellow">

**Marking opens to a few test firms first.** Until then, turn on 1099 tracking in QuickBooks itself. The list works
for every firm.

</AiNote>

<AiNote tone="yellow">

**Using Grok?** Tell it to wait for your yes before it marks anything.

</AiNote>

<AiNote tone="green">

**Tax IDs are never shown.** The list only says whether one is on file. Don't type a tax ID in the chat.

</AiNote>

## More details

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="What happens after you ask">

1. The AI finds the client. LedgerSync reads the year's payments from the client's QuickBooks. For a client with a
   lot of activity, the AI may say LedgerSync is still reading, and asks again about 30 seconds later.
2. The AI shows you the vendors paid at or over the 1099 amount for the year: $2,000 for 2026 payments, $600 for
   2025. For each vendor: how much counts, whether it is marked for 1099 in QuickBooks, and whether a tax ID and an
   email are on file. It also gives its advice, from the expense accounts the vendor's payments were booked to: it
   points to a 1099, points away from one, or asks you, with the reason.
3. It asks you about the vendors the accounts don't settle, one at a time, and about the vendors marked for 1099
   whose accounts point away. You decide.
4. Want only some accounts to count, as in QuickBooks' own 1099 setup? Tell the AI which ones. It lists the vendors
   again, counting only those accounts and the accounts under them, and tells you how much it left out and how many
   vendors dropped off the list. Nothing is saved: tell it again next time.
5. It goes through the vendors not marked for 1099, and the ones with no tax ID on file, with you. A vendor with no
   tax ID on file needs a W-9 before you file.
6. You pick the vendors to mark, or to unmark. The AI shows you a preview: each vendor's 1099 setting before and
   after, and the vendors LedgerSync would leave as they are, with the reason. Nothing has changed yet.
7. You say yes. Claude and ChatGPT also ask you to allow it: allow it if the preview looked right. LedgerSync turns
   on 1099 tracking for each vendor in QuickBooks and changes nothing else on the vendor. A minute or so later, the AI
   tells you which vendors were marked and which were left as they were, and why.
8. Changed your mind? Ask the AI to undo it. It shows which vendors would go back, and puts them back after your yes.
   A vendor someone changed in QuickBooks since is left as it is.
9. At the end, the AI tells you what the list does not cover, and offers a dashboard of the list or the same check
   for your other clients.

<AiNote tone="gray">

**Which payments count?** Checks, cash and bank payments to a vendor, and bill payments, by the date they were paid.
A bill counts when it is paid. Card payments, and payments through PayPal,
Venmo, Square, Stripe or Cash App, are left out: the card company or the app reports them on a 1099-K.

</AiNote>

<AiNote tone="gray">

**How the advice works.** LedgerSync looks at the expense accounts each payment was booked to, and for a bill
payment, the accounts on the bills it paid. It reads each account's name first, then the type QuickBooks gives it.
Services and legal fees point to a 1099-NEC, and rent to a 1099-MISC. Goods, travel, utilities, phone, freight,
insurance, taxes, wages and bank fees point away. Repairs, advertising and catch-all accounts such as Uncategorized
Expense get a question, and so do supplies on a contractor's bill with the work. Each form needs $2,000 on its own
for 2026 payments, so $1,500 of labor and $1,000 of rent to one vendor also get a question. It is advice: you decide.

</AiNote>

<AiNote tone="gray">

**Corporations.** Most corporations need no 1099, but lawyers and medical providers do. QuickBooks cannot tell which
vendors are corporations, so the list keeps them. Check each vendor's W-9. The advice counts legal fees even when
the law firm is a corporation.

</AiNote>

<AiNote tone="gray">

**What changes in QuickBooks.** Only each vendor's 1099 tracking. Nothing else on the vendor changes, and LedgerSync
never shows or changes a tax ID. LedgerSync keeps who said yes, and in which AI app.

</AiNote>

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What it does not cover">

- Royalties.
- Gross proceeds paid to attorneys.
- Medical and health care payments.
- Which 1099 form and box each payment goes in. The AI only suggests a form from each account's name, and your
  client's 1099 setup in QuickBooks may map an account differently.
- Payments entered as journal entries.
- Asking vendors for a W-9.
- Filing. File the 1099s through your usual 1099 service. For 2026 payments, 1099-NEC forms are due February 1, 2027.
- Clients that use another accounting app, such as QuickBooks Desktop or Zoho Books.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Before you start">

- **LedgerSync is in your AI app:** Claude, ChatGPT or Grok. Not yet? Go to [Start here](/ai/start-here). Added it
  a while ago? Do [After a LedgerSync update](/ai/troubleshooting#after-an-update) once, so your AI gets the 1099
  tools.
- **The client is connected to QuickBooks in LedgerSync**, the same connection you use to sync transactions from
  the LedgerSync app.
- **The client uses QuickBooks Online.**

</Accordion>

<AiNote tone="yellow">

**Your client's vendors go through your AI account.** The AI app reads the client's vendors and what each was paid,
and keeps the chat under your AI account's settings. Check that account's data settings before you start. Tax IDs
never leave LedgerSync: the list only says whether one is on file.

</AiNote>

## If something goes wrong

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="Most common messages">

| The AI says | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| It doesn't know how to do this | Your AI may still have an older copy of LedgerSync's tools. Do [After a LedgerSync update](/ai/troubleshooting#after-an-update), then start a new chat. |
| **QuickBooks is not connected for this client in LedgerSync.** | In the LedgerSync app, click **Rules Engine**, then **QuickBooks**. In the menu on the left, click **Manage Connections**. Find the client and click **Authorize**, then sign in to the client's QuickBooks. Then ask the AI again. |
| **The client's QuickBooks connection in LedgerSync stopped working.** | Open **Manage Connections** the same way. At the top, next to the client's name, click **Reconnect** and sign in to QuickBooks again. Then ask the AI again. |
| LedgerSync is still reading the client's payments | Wait. The AI asks again about 30 seconds later. |
| **Marking vendors for 1099 from an AI app is open to a few test firms first; it opens to everyone soon.** | Turn on 1099 tracking for those vendors in QuickBooks itself for now. The list still works. |
| A vendor is inactive in QuickBooks | QuickBooks refuses any change to an inactive vendor. Make it active in QuickBooks, then ask the AI to preview again. |
| A vendor changed in QuickBooks since the preview | LedgerSync left it as it is. Ask the AI to preview it again. |
| **LedgerSync has no default 1099 threshold for** a year | Tell the AI the amount to use for that year, for example 600. |
| **None of these accounts is in the client's QuickBooks**, or **the client's QuickBooks has no account** you chose | Ask the AI to show the client's accounts, then tell it again which ones count. When only some are missing, the AI still shows the list for the others. |

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="More messages: preview expired, change not confirmed, LedgerSync busy, read-only, limits, undo">

| The AI says | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| **This confirmation is unknown or expired. Preview the changes again.** | A preview works for 30 minutes. Ask the AI to preview again. |
| LedgerSync **did not confirm** the change, so it may or may not be made | Don't ask it to mark again. Wait a minute, then ask it to check the status. |
| **These changes are already being applied.** | Wait a minute, then ask the AI for the status. |
| **Other changes are being applied; try again in a minute.** | Nothing was changed. Wait a minute, then ask the AI to mark them again with the same preview. A preview works for 30 minutes. |
| **This connection is read-only or lacks permission for this action.** | This connection can read but not change anything. Turn on 1099 tracking in QuickBooks itself, or email <AiEmail />. |
| This connection has used a tool too many times this hour or this day | Each AI app you connect can preview 60 times an hour, mark 30 times a day and undo 10 times a day. Try again next hour or tomorrow, as the AI says, or do it in QuickBooks itself. |
| It cannot find the client | Ask "Find my client ..." first. Not the firm owner or an administrator? You see only the clients assigned to you in LedgerSync. |
| Some vendors could not be put back | Ask the AI to undo again: it tries those vendors again. |
| The client is now connected to a different QuickBooks company | LedgerSync can undo only while the client is connected to the QuickBooks company the changes were made in. |

</Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>

Anything else? See [Troubleshooting](/ai/troubleshooting).

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="For developers: the 5 tools and what they change">
    | Tool | What it does | Changes data |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | `ledgersync_get_1099_summary` | The vendors a QuickBooks client paid at or over the 1099 threshold in a tax year, each with its QuickBooks 1099 flag, whether a tax ID and an email are on file (never the tax ID itself), a suggested next step, and advice from the expense accounts its payments were booked to; `count_only_account_ids` counts only the accounts the accountant chooses | No |
    | `ledgersync_preview_ledger_changes` | Checks marking up to 100 of the client's vendors for 1099, or unmarking them, and shows each one's 1099 flag before and after | No |
    | `ledgersync_apply_ledger_changes` | Marks or unmarks the previewed vendors for 1099 in the client's QuickBooks, in the background | Yes |
    | `ledgersync_get_ledger_changes_status` | How each vendor's change went, and after an undo how the undo went | No |
    | `ledgersync_undo_ledger_changes` | Shows which flags would go back; after a yes, puts them back | Yes |

    - The instructions above are also an MCP prompt, `form_1099_season` ("Find who needs a 1099"), with optional
      `client` and `tax_year` arguments. Claude Code lists it as a command.
    - Every ready answer of `ledgersync_get_1099_summary` ends its `next_step` with what the list does not cover,
      before its offers, so an AI that never sees the prompt still says it.
    - Each vendor carries `advice` (`points_to_1099`, `points_away` or `ask`) and an `advice_reason`, and the first
      page lists the accounts behind them in `expense_accounts`. The `next_step` asks about the `ask` vendors first,
      then, when some vendors are in question or point away, offers `count_only_account_ids`. Nothing about the
      choice is saved.
    - The list and the status need `read:accounting`; the preview, the apply and the undo need `write:accounting`.
      A read-only connection can read the list but cannot mark vendors.
    - Marking is open to a few test firms first. For any other firm, the preview and the apply answer
      `access_not_active`.
    - Each `vendor_id` in the 1099 list is sealed to its client: the id, a dot and 8 more characters. The preview takes
      it only as the list gave it for that client: one from another client's list, or one with no seal (as
      LedgerSync gave ids before), is refused with `invalid_request`, and its `next_step` tells the AI to list this client's vendors again. The apply
      takes the preview's
      `confirmation_id` with the same `client_id` and `client_name` the preview named.
    - On the sandbox connector, marking really changes the Intuit sandbox QuickBooks company the client is connected
      to. There is no dry run.
    - See [For developers](/ai/developers) for every field.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="For developers: limits">
    - One preview takes up to 100 vendors.
    - Each connected AI app can make 60 previews an hour, 30 applies a day and 10 undos a day. An apply or an undo
      that never reaches LedgerSync is not counted.
    - A preview's confirmation works for 30 minutes, and an undo's `undo_id` for 15 minutes.
    - One set of changes runs at a time for each client.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
