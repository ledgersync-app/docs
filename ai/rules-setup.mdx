## Start

<AiStep id="new-chat" n={1} total={4} title="Start a new chat">

In Claude, ChatGPT or Grok. In ChatGPT, also type **@** and pick **LedgerSync**.

</AiStep>

<AiStep id="ask" n={2} total={4} title="Ask the AI" img="/images/ai-rules/rules-1-ask.png" caption="Your request">

Type *"Set up rules for Acme"*, with your client's name, and press **Enter**.

</AiStep>

<AiStep id="answer" n={3} total={4} title="Say yes or no" img="/images/ai-rules/rules-2-suggestions.png" caption="Rules it suggests">

The AI lists the rules it suggests, each by name. For example: *Square* files Square deposits to Sales Revenue. Say
yes, no, or what to change.

</AiStep>

<AiStep id="preview" n={4} total={4} title="Say yes to the preview" imgs={[{ src: "/images/ai-rules/rules-3-preview.png", caption: "The preview" }, { src: "/images/ai-rules/rules-4-allow.png", caption: "Allow once circled" }]}>

The AI shows what it will save. Say yes, and allow it if Claude or ChatGPT asks. Nothing changes before that.

</AiStep>

<AiNote tone="yellow">

**Using Grok?** It may save without asking. Tell it to wait for your yes.

</AiNote>

<AiNote tone="green">

**Or let the AI offer.** Ask it to show a client's transactions. If some have no rule yet, it offers to suggest
rules.

</AiNote>

<AiNote tone="green">

**Change a rule you have.** Ask, for example: *"Turn off Acme's Shell rule."* The AI shows you a preview first. See
[Changing your rules](#changing-your-rules).

</AiNote>

<AiNote tone="green">

**Then accept the filled transactions.** Ask, for example: *"Move Acme's For review transactions with rules to
Categorized."* The AI shows you a preview first. See [Accept For review transactions](/ai/accept-transactions).

</AiNote>

<AiNote tone="gray">

**Changed your mind?** Ask: *"Undo the rules setup you just did for Acme."* See [Undo](#undo).

</AiNote>

<AiNote tone="yellow">

**Your client's bank data goes through your AI account.** Check its data settings first.

</AiNote>

## More details

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="What happens after you ask">

1. The AI checks the client: a Manual client, or a QuickBooks client and its QuickBooks connection. It reads what
   the client already has: its categories, vendors (and, for a QuickBooks client, its customers) and rules. It also
   reads your firm's global categories, global vendors and global rules.
2. It reads the client's waiting transactions that no rule has filled yet. It groups them by their bank text,
   largest groups first.
3. For each group, it proposes a rule: the words to look for in the bank text, a category and a vendor. It uses the
   categories and vendors you have, or proposes new ones and tells you they are new. It names the rules like your
   existing ones. When it is not sure, it asks you. For transactions that look like they should be left out of the
   books, such as transfers between the client's own accounts, it proposes an exclusion rule instead: the words and a
   category, with no vendor needed.
4. It proposes each rule to you by name, saying what it does and whether it is for this client only or for every
   client of your firm. For example: *Can I create a global rule 'Square' that leaves Square deposits out of the
   books for every client?* You say yes, no, or what to change. You never have to ask for a client rule, a global
   rule or an exclusion rule yourself.
5. It proposes a link for each of your global categories that the client has no category linked to yet, all at
   once. See [Link your global categories](#link-your-global-categories).
6. It shows you a preview: what would be created and linked, how many waiting transactions each rule would fill (or,
   for an exclusion rule, mark for exclusion) for this client and for your other clients, and every warning. For a
   QuickBooks client, it also names your other QuickBooks clients that still need a link. Nothing has changed yet.
7. You say yes. Claude and ChatGPT also ask you to allow it: allow it if the preview looked right.
8. LedgerSync saves the rules, the links, and any new categories and vendors. For a QuickBooks client, it first
   creates the new categories and vendors in the client's QuickBooks. Then it fills the waiting transactions. An
   exclusion rule's transactions are also marked for exclusion: they stay on **For review**, and move to
   **Excluded** when you accept them. The AI checks until it is done. Then it tells you what was created, how many
   transactions were filled, and what failed, with the reason.
9. For a QuickBooks client, the AI then offers to post the filled transactions into QuickBooks, with
   [Bookkeeping into QuickBooks](/ai/quickbooks-bookkeeping).

<AiNote tone="gray">

**Which transactions?** The ones waiting on the client's **For review** list in LedgerSync that no rule has filled
and nobody has given a category. Transactions still pending at the bank, and excluded ones, are left out.

</AiNote>

<AiNote tone="gray">

**Your existing rules stay as they are, unless you ask for a change.** New rules never take a transaction that a rule
already filled or that someone gave a category. New rules go at the end of the list, after the rules you have. If
one of your existing rules matches one of this client's waiting transactions that it had not filled yet, LedgerSync
fills that one too. At a QuickBooks client, that includes the ones your global rules can file once a new link is
saved. The preview and the result show how many. To change a rule you have, see
[Changing your rules](#changing-your-rules).

</AiNote>

<AiNote tone="gray">

**A big setup goes in batches.** One preview takes up to 100 rules and 50 changes to existing rules. For more, the
AI works in batches. Each batch has its own preview and needs your yes.

</AiNote>

<AiNote tone="yellow">

**Give each rule a vendor.** A rule puts its vendor on each transaction it fills, as the payee. In LedgerSync you can
accept a transaction only when it has a payee, so for a rule without a vendor you add the payee yourself before you
accept. The AI gives every rule a vendor. To pick one yourself, tell the AI. For example: *Use Shell as the vendor.*
For a QuickBooks client, a client rule's payee can also be one of the client's customers. An exclusion rule needs no
vendor: accepting its transactions moves them to **Excluded**.

</AiNote>

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Client rules and global rules">

The AI picks the kind of each rule and says it when it proposes the rule: a global rule for vendors that mean the
same for every client, such as bank fees, software or fuel, and a client rule otherwise. If your login cannot make
global rules, it proposes client rules only. You decide: say yes, or ask for the other kind.

| | Client rule | Global rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Works for | This client only | Every client of your firm with the same accounting app: your Manual clients, or your QuickBooks clients |
| Puts the transaction in | One of the client's categories | One of your firm's global categories (at a QuickBooks client, through the client's own category linked to it) |
| Payee | One of the client's vendors or, at a QuickBooks client, one of its customers | One of your firm's global vendors |
| Fills when it is saved | This client's waiting transactions | This client's waiting transactions, and the ones at your other clients with the same accounting app |
| After that | This client's new transactions | New transactions at every client of your firm with the same accounting app |

- A client's own rules always come first. A global rule fills a transaction only when none of that client's rules
  match it.
- To make or change global rules, your login needs **Manage Global Rules**, as in **Before you start**.
- Either kind can be an exclusion rule, as in the LedgerSync app. A global exclusion rule at a QuickBooks client
  works only through the client's category linked to its global category, like any global rule.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Link your global categories" id="link-your-global-categories">

A global rule puts the transaction in one of your firm's global categories. Each client can link one of its own
categories to each global category. What the link does depends on the client:

- **QuickBooks client:** the link is how a global rule files the client's transactions. The rule puts each one in
  the client's QuickBooks category that is linked to the global category. Without a link, the rule cannot file it:
  the transaction waits on **For review** with a red badge showing the rule's name, until the category is linked.
  The AI calls it the "map this category" badge.
- **Manual client:** linked, LedgerSync uses the GL code of the client's own category, in the app and in the
  exports. The category name stays the global one, shown with that GL code in front. If the client's category has
  no GL code, LedgerSync uses the global one. Not linked, LedgerSync uses the global category and its GL code.

**The AI proposes all the missing links at once.** It goes through every global category the client has no link
for, starting with the ones the client's waiting transactions need, then the ones your global rules use. For each
one, it looks for the client's category that fits:

- **The client's history shows it.** The client's past transactions that the category's global rules match were
  booked to one of the client's categories (at least 3 of them, and at least 8 in 10). The AI proposes that one.
- **A best guess.** One of the client's categories has the same name as the global category, or the same name as
  the category your other clients link it to. The AI proposes its best guess and shows you the other options.
- **How the client's QuickBooks booked them.** At a QuickBooks client, LedgerSync also looks at the client's expenses
  and deposits of the last 12 months in QuickBooks. When nothing above fits, and the category's global rules match at
  least 3 of them with at least 8 in 10 booked to one of the client's categories, the AI proposes that one. When it
  disagrees with a best guess, the AI shows it as another option. Transactions LedgerSync sent to QuickBooks itself,
  and money moved to or from a bank or credit card account, do not count.
- **Nothing to go on.** The AI asks you. At a QuickBooks client, it asks only about the links that transactions are
  waiting for. It tells you how many others can wait: link them later, or ask for them now.

It puts every link it can into one preview and asks you about the rest. Check them: say yes, or tell the AI which
ones are wrong. For example: *Link Fuel to Auto Expense, not to Fuel Surcharge.* At a QuickBooks client, the
preview also says how many waiting transactions your global rules would fill once each link is saved.

To ask for one link yourself, tell the AI. For example: *Link our global category Fuel to Acme's category Auto
Fuel.* LedgerSync saves the links after your yes, marked LS-AI. A link works when the client has no link yet for
that global category, and the client's category is not linked to another global category. At a QuickBooks client,
the category must also be active in QuickBooks. One preview takes up to 100 links.

**Link one global category at every client.** Ask the AI. For example: *Link our global category Fuel at every
client.* The AI also offers this when it finds other clients that still need a link.

1. The AI finds every client that has no category linked to the global category: your QuickBooks clients for a
   QuickBooks global category, your Manual clients for a Manual one. For each client, it proposes a category the
   same way as above: from the client's history, a best guess, or nothing to go on.
2. It shows you the totals and a summary of the categories it would link. For example: *Fuel at 120 clients,
   Auto:Fuel at 40, nothing to go on at 30.* Then it shows the clients that need your answer. At QuickBooks
   clients, it also says how many waiting transactions your global rules would file once the links are saved.
   Nothing has changed yet.
3. You say yes, or name the clients to change. For example: *For Acme, use Fuel Surcharge. Leave Beta out.* The AI
   checks again with your changes. A client with nothing to go on gets no link unless you name its category. When
   you say yes, Claude and ChatGPT also ask you to allow it.
4. After your yes, LedgerSync links the category client by client, in the background. Each link is marked LS-AI.
   At each QuickBooks client, your global rules then file the transactions that were waiting for this link, and
   their "map this category" badge goes away. If one client fails, the others still get their link. The AI tells
   you where it linked, how many transactions were filed, and what failed, with the reason.

- **One undo takes it all back.** Ask the AI to undo it, as for any setup. It takes back every link of that setup.
  At QuickBooks clients, the transactions filed through those links go back to waiting, with the badge where no
  other rule takes them. A link someone changed since stays as it is. Only someone who sees all of those clients in
  LedgerSync can undo it.
- **Your clients only.** An employee sees only the clients assigned to them. The AI links the category only at
  those, and only counts the others.
- **Limits.** One preview links one global category, at up to 1,000 clients. With more, say yes to the first
  preview, and once it is done, ask the AI again for the rest. You can name up to 500 clients to change and 500 to
  leave out. If your global rules for that category have very many conditions, the AI may not have time to read
  every client's history. For those clients, it makes a best guess or asks you.

You can also link them in the LedgerSync app. Click **Rules Engine**, then **Manual** or **QuickBooks**. In the menu
on the left, click **Settings**, then **Initial Setup**, then **Categories Mapping**. This opens the list of global
categories. Click the global category. Under **Connect Client Categories**, pick the client's category next to the
client's name, then click **Save**.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="QuickBooks clients">

For a QuickBooks Online client, rules setup works the same way, with these differences.

- **The client's categories and vendors are its QuickBooks ones.** A client rule puts the transaction in one of the
  client's QuickBooks categories. Its payee is one of the client's QuickBooks vendors or customers, never an
  employee. A global rule uses your firm's global categories and global vendors, as for any client.
- **New categories and vendors are created in the client's QuickBooks, after your yes.** LedgerSync creates them
  before it saves the rules. When QuickBooks already has one with that name, LedgerSync uses it instead: for a
  category, only when it is of the same type and in the same place, under the same category or at the top level.
  LedgerSync never turns a deactivated QuickBooks category or vendor back on, so a name that belongs to one is
  refused, and the AI tells you which.
- **A new category needs a QuickBooks account type**, such as Expense or Income, unless it goes under another
  category. A setup never creates customers.
- **A new category goes at the top level, or under one of the client's categories.** For example: *Make a new
  category Fuel under Auto.* QuickBooks calls it a sub-account. It gets the type of the category it goes under. The
  preview, the result and the undo show it with that category's name in front: *Auto:Fuel*. The preview checks that
  category in the client's QuickBooks first: it must be active. QuickBooks allows 5 levels at most, and never puts a
  category under a few special ones, such as Undeposited Funds, Opening Balance Equity or Retained Earnings.
- **A new category can also have a QuickBooks detail type**, such as Travel or Office/General Administrative
  Expenses. It must be one LedgerSync knows for the category's account type: if not, the AI names some that are.
  Under another category, a new one with no detail type gets that category's. At the top level, the AI gives
  every new category a detail type, since QuickBooks would otherwise pick its own (Travel, for an expense
  category). The preview shows the detail type each new category gets, before you say yes.
- **The preview names your other QuickBooks clients that need a link.** A new global rule files transactions at
  your other QuickBooks clients only where they link its global category, and so does a global rule you edit or
  turn on. At the others, those transactions wait with the badge. The preview counts them, and names those clients
  if you can see them in LedgerSync (an employee sees the clients assigned to them). To link the category there, ask
  the AI to link it at every client, as in [Link your global categories](#link-your-global-categories), or link it
  in the LedgerSync app.
- **If the client's QuickBooks connection stopped working,** the AI can still read what the client has (except its
  customers), make links and make rules. But the client's new categories, its new vendors and customers as payees
  need QuickBooks: the preview refuses them and asks you to reconnect QuickBooks first. To reconnect, click
  **Rules Engine**, then **QuickBooks** in the LedgerSync app. In the menu on the left, click
  **Settings**, then **Client Settings**, then **Manage Connections**. Click **Reconnect** next to the client and
  sign in to QuickBooks again. Then ask the AI to preview again.
- **Then post the transactions into QuickBooks.** The setup fills the transactions in LedgerSync only, on
  **For review**. To post them into the client's QuickBooks from the same chat, use
  [Bookkeeping into QuickBooks](/ai/quickbooks-bookkeeping), for accounts with no QuickBooks bank feed. The AI keeps
  the category and payee your rules set. Or accept and sync them in the LedgerSync app.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Changing your rules" id="changing-your-rules">

Your AI can also change the rules you already have, for Manual and QuickBooks Online clients. Ask it in your own
words. For example: *Turn off Acme's Shell rule*, *Change Acme's Amazon rule to Office Supplies*, *Move Acme's Uber
rule to the top* or *Delete our global Zoom rule*.

1. The AI finds the rule and reads all of it: its conditions, category, vendor, whether it is on, and its place in
   the list.
2. It shows you a preview: the rule before and after the change, and what happens to the client's waiting
   transactions. Nothing has changed yet.
3. You say yes. Claude and ChatGPT also ask you to allow it. LedgerSync changes the rule, then its waiting
   transactions, and the AI tells you what changed.

| You ask to | The rule | Its waiting transactions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Edit it | Gets the name, money in or out, conditions, category or vendor you asked for. The rest stays as it is. | The ones it still matches get its new category and vendor. The others go back to your other rules. It can also fill waiting transactions that no rule has filled yet. |
| Turn it off | Stays in the list, turned off. | They go back to your other rules. |
| Turn it on | Is turned on again. | It fills the waiting transactions it matches that no rule has filled yet, as a new rule does. |
| Move it | Goes before or after another rule, or first or last in the list. | They keep their rule. New transactions follow the new order. |
| Delete it | Is deleted. | They go back to your other rules. |

- **Transactions that go back are filed again at once.** Your other rules decide them the way they decide new
  transactions. The ones no other rule matches wait on **For review** with no category. The preview names the rules
  that take them.
- **Transactions someone changed stay as they are.** A change touches only the transactions the rule filled that
  nobody gave another category or payee since, and that are not posted yet. The ones someone changed that way, and
  the ones you already moved to **Categorized**, stay as they are, and so do the ones on **Excluded** that an
  exclusion rule marked. The preview counts the ones someone changed.
- **Transactions you excluded yourself are changed too.** When a rule that is not an exclusion rule filled a
  transaction that someone then excluded, by hand or with its bank account, a change to that rule changes it like a
  waiting one. It stays on **Excluded**, but the change takes the rule's category and vendor off it, or gives it the
  edited rule's, and your other rules do not file it again.
- **Global rules change at every client.** A change to a global rule works at every client of your firm with the
  same accounting app. Turning one off, deleting it, or editing it so it no longer matches puts back its waiting
  transactions there, and each client's other rules file them again. Turning one on fills waiting transactions here
  and at your other clients, as a new global rule does. The preview shows what happens at your other clients.
  Changing global rules needs **Manage Global Rules**.
- **Global rules at your QuickBooks clients.** Turning a global rule off, deleting it or editing it also takes off
  its "map this category" badges. Those transactions are filed again at once, by each client's other rules or by the
  edited rule. A global rule you edit or turn on files transactions only at the clients that link its global
  category. At the others, the transactions it matches wait with the badge. The preview counts them, and names those
  clients, as for a new global rule. To delete a global rule the LedgerSync app is still applying to transactions,
  the setup waits up to about 15 minutes for that to finish.
- **Global rules that share a priority.** When some of your global rules have the same **Priority** number in the
  LedgerSync app, the order between them is not certain. So the AI cannot move a global rule across them or next to
  them, or delete one of them. Ask the AI for another place, or move or delete the rule in the LedgerSync app.
- **Exclusion rules change the same way.** You can change an exclusion rule like any other rule. It stays an
  exclusion rule: the waiting transactions it keeps or fills are marked for exclusion, and move to **Excluded**
  when you accept them. The preview and the result count them with the change, apart from what new exclusion rules
  mark. A change cannot make a rule an exclusion rule, or the other way around.
- **Rules can look at the amount.** A condition can also check the amount: equals, doesn't equal, is greater than or
  is less than. For example: *Make a rule for Shell over 500*. Write a plain amount, such as 1200.50, with no $ sign,
  commas or minus sign. The amount is compared without its sign: money in or money out is set on the rule. A rule
  still needs words to look for in the bank text. This works in new rules too.
- **Changes and new rules can go together.** One preview takes up to 50 changes, one per rule, along with new rules.
- **If a rule changes after the preview,** for example in the LedgerSync app, LedgerSync does not make the change to
  it. If it finds this before it saves anything, nothing is saved. If it finds it while it saves, only that change
  fails, and the rest is saved. Either way, ask the AI to read the rule again and preview the change again.
- **A changed rule is not marked LS-AI.** Its history shows the change instead: in the LedgerSync app, open the rule
  and click the **Rule History** button at the top right. A deleted rule has no page to open its history from. A
  QuickBooks global rule the AI edits also gets a line on the **Apply Status** tab of **Global Rules**: **Finished**,
  with 0 categorized transactions, because the setup files its transactions itself. Undoing the edit adds one more.
- **Changed your mind?** Undo the setup, as for new rules. It puts the rules back as they were, and their waiting
  transactions too. See [Undo](#undo).

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What LedgerSync saves">

- The rules. Each one looks for words in the bank text (contains, doesn't contain or is exactly) and, if you want,
  at the amount, for money in, money out or both. It puts a category on the transaction and its vendor as the payee.
- New categories and vendors, for the client or for your firm (global). For a QuickBooks client, the client's new
  categories and vendors are also created in its QuickBooks. Global ones live in LedgerSync only.
- The changes to your existing rules that you said yes to, and what they do to the rules' waiting transactions, as
  in [Changing your rules](#changing-your-rules).
- Links between one of the client's categories and a global category, where the client has no link for either one
  yet.
- The category on each waiting transaction the rules fill and, when the rule has a vendor, that vendor as its payee.
  The transactions stay on **For review**, as when your own rules fill them.
- For an exclusion rule, the waiting transactions it fills are also marked for exclusion, and stay on **For review**.
  In the LedgerSync app they are highlighted, and the **Exclusion Rule** filter shows them. When you accept them,
  they move to **Excluded** instead of **Categorized**, and they are never posted to QuickBooks.

<AiNote tone="green">

**Everything the AI makes is marked LS-AI.** LedgerSync marks each rule, category, vendor and link the AI creates
**LS-AI**, with the setup it came from, so you can tell it apart from your own setup. LedgerSync also records who
asked for the setup and from which AI app. To see what the AI made, ask it. For example: *Show me the rules you set
up for Acme.*

</AiNote>

**Where to find them in the LedgerSync app.** Click **Rules Engine**, then **Manual** or **QuickBooks**. In the menu
on the left, the rules are under **Rules**: **Client Rules** and **Global Rules**. Categories and vendors are under
**Settings**: **Client Settings** has the client's (its categories and its vendors), and **Global Settings** has
your firm's (**Global Categories**, **Global Vendors**). A QuickBooks client's new categories and vendors are also
in its QuickBooks.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Undo">

Changed your mind? Ask the AI to undo the setup. For example: *Undo the rules setup you just did for Acme.*

1. The AI first checks what the undo would do. Claude and ChatGPT already ask you to allow it here: allow it,
   because this check changes nothing. Then the AI shows you what the undo would remove and what it would keep,
   and why. Nothing has changed yet.
2. You say yes. Claude and ChatGPT also ask you to allow it.
3. LedgerSync deletes the rules the setup made. It takes their category and payee (and, for an exclusion rule, its
   mark) off the waiting transactions they filled. When the setup had no changes to your existing rules, that
   includes a payee or category you changed yourself on one of them. When it had some, a transaction you changed
   yourself stays as it is: see **When the setup changed your existing rules** below.
4. Transactions you moved to **Excluded** because of the setup's exclusion rules go back to **For review**. The AI
   says how many before you say yes.
5. It undoes the category links the setup made.
6. It deletes the categories and vendors the setup made, unless something uses them now, such as another rule, a
   transaction or a linked category. Those stay, and the AI tells you which.

For a QuickBooks client, three things are different:

- **The categories and vendors the setup created in QuickBooks stay**, in QuickBooks and in LedgerSync. LedgerSync
  never deletes anything in QuickBooks. The AI tells you which ones stay. A category the setup created loses the
  link the setup gave it.
- **The transactions your global rules filed through a link the setup made go back to waiting**, even ones filed
  after the setup. Where no other rule takes them, they get the "map this category" badge again.
- **The "map this category" badges the setup's global rules put on transactions go away**, at this client and at
  your other QuickBooks clients.

**When the setup changed your existing rules.** The undo also puts back what the setup changed, as it was before the
setup:

- **The rules.** Each rule goes back to how it was: its name, conditions, category, vendor, whether it is on, and its
  place in the list. A rule the setup deleted comes back, in its old place or as close to it as it can.
- **The transactions.** Each waiting transaction the setup changed goes back to how it was before the setup. That
  includes the ones it put back for your other rules, and the ones the rules filled during the setup.
- **Global rules, at every client.** A global rule the setup changed goes back the same way, a deleted one included,
  and so do its transactions at every client of your firm. A deleted global rule's earlier lines on the
  **Apply Status** tab of **Global Rules** do not come back.
- **What changed since stays.** A rule someone changed after the setup stays as it is now, and so does a rule whose
  old category was deleted. A moved rule goes back to its old place, or between its old neighbours. If those were
  moved apart since, it stays where it is. A transaction someone changed after the setup stays as it is too, even
  one the setup's new rules filled. Transactions you moved to **Excluded** because of the setup's new exclusion
  rules still go back to **For review**, as in step 4 above. Before you say yes, the AI tells you which.
- **A later setup goes first.** If a later setup changed the same rules or transactions, undo that one first. The AI
  offers to.

<AiNote tone="gray">

**An undo never changes** the transactions you already moved to **Categorized**, or your own rules, except the ones
the setup changed. When the setup had no changes to your rules, it also leaves alone the transactions your existing
rules filled during the setup, except, at a QuickBooks client, the ones your global rules filed through a link the
setup made.

</AiNote>

An undo works once the setup is no longer running, whether it is done, partly done or failed. It removes only what
the setup saved, and puts back only what it changed. If an undo could not finish, ask the AI to undo the setup again.
While another setup of your firm is running or being undone, an undo cannot start: ask the AI again when that one
finishes. Undoing global rules, global categories, global vendors or changes to global rules needs
**Manage Global Rules**.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What it does not do yet">

- Clients that use another accounting app, such as Zoho Books.
- Changing the category of a rule that splits each transaction over several categories.
