## More details

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="What happens after you ask">

1. The AI checks the client: a Manual client, or a QuickBooks client and its QuickBooks connection. It reads what
   the client already has: its categories, vendors (and, for a QuickBooks client, its customers) and rules. It also
   reads your firm's global categories, global vendors and global rules.
2. It reads the client's waiting transactions that no rule has filled yet. It groups them by their bank text,
   largest groups first.
3. For each group, it proposes a rule: the words to look for in the bank text, a category and a vendor. It uses the
   categories and vendors you have, or proposes new ones and tells you they are new. It names the rules like your
   existing ones. When it is not sure, it asks you. For transactions that look like they should be left out of the
   books, such as transfers between the client's own accounts, it proposes an exclusion rule instead: the words and a
   category, with no vendor needed.
4. It proposes each rule to you by name, saying what it does and whether it is for this client only or for every
   client of your firm. For example: *Can I create a global rule 'Square' that leaves Square deposits out of the
   books for every client?* You say yes, no, or what to change. You never have to ask for a client rule, a global
   rule or an exclusion rule yourself.
5. It proposes a link for each of your global categories that the client has no category linked to yet, all at
   once. See [Link your global categories](#link-your-global-categories).
6. It shows you a preview: what would be created and linked, how many waiting transactions each rule would fill (or,
   for an exclusion rule, mark for exclusion) for this client and for your other clients, and every warning. For a
   QuickBooks client, it also names your other QuickBooks clients that still need a link. Nothing has changed yet.
7. You say yes. Claude and ChatGPT also ask you to allow it: allow it if the preview looked right.
8. LedgerSync saves the rules, the links, and any new categories and vendors. For a QuickBooks client, it first
   creates the new categories and vendors in the client's QuickBooks. Then it fills the waiting transactions. An
   exclusion rule's transactions are also marked for exclusion: they stay on **For review**, and move to
   **Excluded** when you accept them. The AI checks until it is done. Then it tells you what was created, how many
   transactions were filled, and what failed, with the reason.
9. For a QuickBooks client, the AI then offers to post the filled transactions into QuickBooks, with
   [Bookkeeping into QuickBooks](/ai/quickbooks-bookkeeping).

<AiNote tone="gray">

**Which transactions?** The ones waiting on the client's **For review** list in LedgerSync that no rule has filled
and nobody has given a category. Transactions still pending at the bank, and excluded ones, are left out.

</AiNote>

<AiNote tone="gray">

**Your existing rules stay as they are.** LedgerSync never changes a transaction that a rule already filled or that
someone gave a category. New rules go at the end of the list, after the rules you have. If one of your existing
rules matches one of this client's waiting transactions that it had not filled yet, LedgerSync fills that one too.
At a QuickBooks client, that includes the ones your global rules can file once a new link is saved. The preview and
the result show how many.

</AiNote>

<AiNote tone="gray">

**A big setup goes in batches.** One preview takes up to 100 rules. For more, the AI works in batches. Each batch
has its own preview and needs your yes.

</AiNote>

<AiNote tone="yellow">

**Give each rule a vendor.** A rule puts its vendor on each transaction it fills, as the payee. In LedgerSync you can
accept a transaction only when it has a payee, so for a rule without a vendor you add the payee yourself before you
accept. The AI gives every rule a vendor. To pick one yourself, tell the AI. For example: *Use Shell as the vendor.*
For a QuickBooks client, a client rule's payee can also be one of the client's customers. An exclusion rule needs no
vendor: accepting its transactions moves them to **Excluded**.

</AiNote>

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Client rules and global rules">

The AI picks the kind of each rule and says it when it proposes the rule: a global rule for vendors that mean the
same for every client, such as bank fees, software or fuel, and a client rule otherwise. If your login cannot make
global rules, it proposes client rules only. You decide: say yes, or ask for the other kind.

| | Client rule | Global rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Works for | This client only | Every client of your firm with the same accounting app: your Manual clients, or your QuickBooks clients |
| Puts the transaction in | One of the client's categories | One of your firm's global categories (at a QuickBooks client, through the client's own category linked to it) |
| Payee | One of the client's vendors or, at a QuickBooks client, one of its customers | One of your firm's global vendors |
| Fills when it is saved | This client's waiting transactions | This client's waiting transactions, and the ones at your other clients with the same accounting app |
| After that | This client's new transactions | New transactions at every client of your firm with the same accounting app |

- A client's own rules always come first. A global rule fills a transaction only when none of that client's rules
  match it.
- To make global rules, your login needs **Manage Global Rules**, as in **Before you start**.
- Either kind can be an exclusion rule, as in the LedgerSync app. A global exclusion rule at a QuickBooks client
  works only through the client's category linked to its global category, like any global rule.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Link your global categories">

A global rule puts the transaction in one of your firm's global categories. Each client can link one of its own
categories to each global category. What the link does depends on the client:

- **QuickBooks client:** the link is how a global rule files the client's transactions. The rule puts each one in
  the client's QuickBooks category that is linked to the global category. Without a link, the rule cannot file it:
  the transaction waits on **For review** with a red badge showing the rule's name, until the category is linked.
  The AI calls it the "map this category" badge.
- **Manual client:** linked, LedgerSync uses the GL code of the client's own category, in the app and in the
  exports. The category name stays the global one, shown with that GL code in front. If the client's category has
  no GL code, LedgerSync uses the global one. Not linked, LedgerSync uses the global category and its GL code.

**The AI proposes all the missing links at once.** It goes through every global category the client has no link
for, starting with the ones your global rules use. For each one, it looks for the client's category that fits:

- **The client's history shows it.** The client's past transactions that the category's global rules match were
  booked to one of the client's categories (at least 3 of them, and at least 8 in 10). The AI proposes that one.
- **A best guess.** One of the client's categories has the same name as the global category, or the same name as
  the category your other clients link it to. The AI proposes its best guess and shows you the other options.
- **Nothing to go on.** The AI asks you.

It puts every link it can into one preview and asks you about the rest. Check them: say yes, or tell the AI which
ones are wrong. For example: *Link Fuel to Auto Expense, not to Fuel Surcharge.* At a QuickBooks client, the
preview also says how many waiting transactions your global rules would fill once each link is saved.

To ask for one link yourself, tell the AI. For example: *Link our global category Fuel to Acme's category Auto
Fuel.* LedgerSync saves the links after your yes, marked LS-AI. A link works when the client has no link yet for
that global category, and the client's category is not linked to another global category. At a QuickBooks client,
the category must also be active in QuickBooks. One preview takes up to 100 links.

You can also link them in the LedgerSync app. Click **Rules Engine**, then **Manual** or **QuickBooks**. In the menu
on the left, click **Settings**, then **Initial Setup**, then **Categories Mapping**. This opens the list of global
categories. Click the global category. Under **Connect Client Categories**, pick the client's category next to the
client's name, then click **Save**.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="QuickBooks clients">

For a QuickBooks Online client, rules setup works the same way, with these differences.

- **The client's categories and vendors are its QuickBooks ones.** A client rule puts the transaction in one of the
  client's QuickBooks categories. Its payee is one of the client's QuickBooks vendors or customers, never an
  employee. A global rule uses your firm's global categories and global vendors, as for any client.
- **New categories and vendors are created in the client's QuickBooks, after your yes.** LedgerSync creates them
  before it saves the rules. When QuickBooks already has one with that name, LedgerSync uses it instead: for a
  category, only when it is of the same type and not under another category. LedgerSync never turns a deactivated
  QuickBooks category or vendor back on, so a name that belongs to one is refused, and the AI tells you which.
- **A new category needs a QuickBooks account type**, such as Expense or Income. It is created at the top level, not
  under another category. A setup never creates customers.
- **The preview names your other QuickBooks clients that need a link.** A new global rule files transactions at
  your other QuickBooks clients only where they link its global category. At the others, those transactions wait
  with the badge. The preview counts them, and names those clients if you can see them in LedgerSync (an employee
  sees the clients assigned to them). To link the category there, set up rules for those clients with the AI too,
  or link it in the LedgerSync app.
- **If the client's QuickBooks connection stopped working,** the AI can still read what the client has (except its
  customers), make links and make rules. But the client's new categories, its new vendors and customers as payees
  need QuickBooks: the preview refuses them and asks you to reconnect QuickBooks first. To reconnect, click
  **Rules Engine**, then **QuickBooks** in the LedgerSync app. In the menu on the left, click
  **Settings**, then **Client Settings**, then **Manage Connections**. Click **Reconnect** next to the client and
  sign in to QuickBooks again. Then ask the AI to preview again.
- **Then post the transactions into QuickBooks.** The setup fills the transactions in LedgerSync only, on
  **For review**. To post them into the client's QuickBooks from the same chat, use
  [Bookkeeping into QuickBooks](/ai/quickbooks-bookkeeping), for accounts with no QuickBooks bank feed. The AI keeps
  the category and payee your rules set. Or accept and sync them in the LedgerSync app.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What LedgerSync saves">

- The rules. Each one looks for words in the bank text (contains, doesn't contain or is exactly), for money in,
  money out or both. It puts a category on the transaction and its vendor as the payee.
- New categories and vendors, for the client or for your firm (global). For a QuickBooks client, the client's new
  categories and vendors are also created in its QuickBooks. Global ones live in LedgerSync only.
- Links between one of the client's categories and a global category, where the client has no link for either one
  yet.
- The category on each waiting transaction the rules fill and, when the rule has a vendor, that vendor as its payee.
  The transactions stay on **For review**, as when your own rules fill them.
- For an exclusion rule, the waiting transactions it fills are also marked for exclusion, and stay on **For review**.
  In the LedgerSync app they are highlighted, and the **Exclusion Rule** filter shows them. When you accept them,
  they move to **Excluded** instead of **Categorized**, and they are never posted to QuickBooks.

<AiNote tone="green">

**Everything the AI makes is marked LS-AI.** LedgerSync marks each rule, category, vendor and link the AI creates
**LS-AI**, with the setup it came from, so you can tell it apart from your own setup. LedgerSync also records who
asked for the setup and from which AI app. To see what the AI made, ask it. For example: *Show me the rules you set
up for Acme.*

</AiNote>

**Where to find them in the LedgerSync app.** Click **Rules Engine**, then **Manual** or **QuickBooks**. In the menu
on the left, the rules are under **Rules**: **Client Rules** and **Global Rules**. Categories and vendors are under
**Settings**: **Client Settings** has the client's (its categories and its vendors), and **Global Settings** has
your firm's (**Global Categories**, **Global Vendors**). A QuickBooks client's new categories and vendors are also
in its QuickBooks.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Undo">

Changed your mind? Ask the AI to undo the setup. For example: *Undo the rules setup you just did for Acme.*

1. The AI first checks what the undo would do. Claude and ChatGPT already ask you to allow it here: allow it,
   because this check changes nothing. Then the AI shows you what the undo would remove and what it would keep,
   and why. Nothing has changed yet.
2. You say yes. Claude and ChatGPT also ask you to allow it.
3. LedgerSync deletes the rules the setup made. It takes their category and payee (and, for an exclusion rule, its
   mark) off the waiting transactions they filled, even a payee or category you changed yourself on one of them.
4. Transactions you moved to **Excluded** because of the setup's exclusion rules go back to **For review**. The AI
   says how many before you say yes.
5. It undoes the category links the setup made.
6. It deletes the categories and vendors the setup made, unless something uses them now, such as another rule, a
   transaction or a linked category. Those stay, and the AI tells you which.

For a QuickBooks client, three things are different:

- **The categories and vendors the setup created in QuickBooks stay**, in QuickBooks and in LedgerSync. LedgerSync
  never deletes anything in QuickBooks. The AI tells you which ones stay. A category the setup created loses the
  link the setup gave it.
- **The transactions your global rules filed through a link the setup made go back to waiting**, even ones filed
  after the setup. Where no other rule takes them, they get the "map this category" badge again.
- **The "map this category" badges the setup's global rules put on transactions go away**, at this client and at
  your other QuickBooks clients.

<AiNote tone="gray">

**An undo never changes** the transactions you already moved to **Categorized**, or your own rules. It also leaves
alone the transactions your existing rules filled during the setup, except, at a QuickBooks client, the ones your
global rules filed through a link the setup made.

</AiNote>

An undo works once the setup is no longer running, whether it is done, partly done or failed. It removes only what
the setup saved. If an undo could not finish, ask the AI to undo the setup again. While another setup of your firm is
running or being undone, an undo cannot start: ask the AI again when that one finishes. Undoing global rules, global
categories or global vendors needs **Manage Global Rules**.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What it does not do yet">

- Clients that use another accounting app, such as Zoho Books.
- Changing, turning off or deleting your existing rules. It only adds new ones.
- Rules on the amount, such as *over $500*. A rule looks at the bank text only.
- New QuickBooks categories under another category, or with a detail type. New ones are made at the top level.
- Employees as payees, and new customers.
- Turning a deactivated QuickBooks category or vendor back on.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Before you start">

- **LedgerSync is in your AI app:** Claude, ChatGPT or Grok. Not yet? Go to [Start here](/ai/start-here). Added it
  a while ago? Do [After a LedgerSync update](/ai/troubleshooting#after-an-update) once, so your AI gets the new
  rules tools.
- **The client is a Manual client or a QuickBooks Online client.** A Manual client is not set up with an accounting
  app in LedgerSync. A QuickBooks client is connected to QuickBooks in LedgerSync. In the LedgerSync app, you work on
  these clients under **Rules Engine**, then **Manual** or **QuickBooks**.
- **The client does not need its own categories yet.** The AI can propose new ones, or use your firm's global
  categories.
- **Global rules need permission.** To make a global rule, your LedgerSync login needs **Manage Global Rules**.
  The firm owner and administrators always have it. Employees have it unless the owner or an administrator turned
  it off. They do that in the LedgerSync app: they click their name at the top, then **Permissions**.

</Accordion>

## If something goes wrong

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="Most common messages">

| The AI says | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| It doesn't know how to do this | Your AI may still have an older copy of LedgerSync's tools. Do [After a LedgerSync update](/ai/troubleshooting#after-an-update), then start a new chat. |
| It cannot make an exclusion rule | Your AI may still have an older copy of LedgerSync's tools. Do [After a LedgerSync update](/ai/troubleshooting#after-an-update), then start a new chat. |
| An exclusion rule's transactions are still on **For review** | That is how exclusion rules work: the rule marks them, and you decide. Accept them in LedgerSync to move them to **Excluded**. |
| The client's QuickBooks connection stopped working | Links and rules still work. The client's new categories, its new vendors and customers as payees need QuickBooks: reconnect it, as in [QuickBooks clients](#quickbooks-clients), then ask the AI to preview again. |
| QuickBooks could not be read just now | Links and rules still work. The client's new categories, its new vendors and customers as payees need QuickBooks: wait a few minutes, then ask the AI to preview again. If it keeps happening, reconnect QuickBooks, as in [QuickBooks clients](#quickbooks-clients). |
| The client uses another accounting app, such as Zoho Books | Not yet. Set up its rules in the LedgerSync app. |
| It cannot find the client | Ask "Find my client ..." first. Not the firm owner or an administrator? You see only the clients assigned to you in LedgerSync. |
| You cannot make global rules | Your LedgerSync login needs **Manage Global Rules**. Make it a client rule, or ask your firm owner. |
| Some items were refused in the preview | The AI says why for each one. Answer its questions, or leave them out, and ask it to preview again. Nothing has changed yet. |
| Some items failed | The AI says which ones and why. The rest was saved. Fix the reason, then ask the AI to preview the missing ones again. |
| QuickBooks did not answer while the setup ran, or its connection stopped working | The setup keeps running, and LedgerSync tries again every few minutes, 5 tries in all. If the connection stopped working, reconnect it. After the last try, what still needs QuickBooks fails, and the rest of the setup is saved: ask the AI to preview the failed items again. |

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Warnings in the preview: name taken, rule fills nothing, overlapping rules, broad rules, no GL code, missing links">

| The AI says | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| A name is already taken | LedgerSync already has a category, vendor or rule with that name. For a QuickBooks client, that includes the categories and vendors in its QuickBooks. The AI can use the one you have, or pick another name. |
| A rule fills no transactions | Its words match no waiting transaction, here or at your other clients, or every transaction they match already goes to another rule (see the next row). Check the words with the AI, or leave the rule out. |
| Another rule already gets a rule's transactions | One of your existing rules, a rule earlier in the preview or, for a global rule, a new client rule fills them first (a client's own rules always come before global rules). Leave the new rule out, or ask the AI for more specific words. |
| Two new rules match the same transactions | Each transaction goes to only one rule: a client rule before a global rule, otherwise the one listed first. Check with the AI that the right rule gets them, or make the words more specific. |
| The words are found inside longer words | For example, *Shell* also matches *Shellfish Grill*. Check the examples. If they are wrong, ask the AI for more specific words. |
| A rule is very broad | It fills more than half of this client's waiting transactions, or more than 200 at your other clients. Make sure it is not too general. |
| The category has no GL code | Manual clients only. The transactions it fills get a category with no GL code. Pick a category that has one, or go ahead if that is fine. |
| The client has no category linked to the global category | At a Manual client, the transactions get the global category and its GL code: to use the GL code of one of the client's categories instead, ask the AI to link it in the same setup. At a QuickBooks client, the rule cannot file them, so they wait with the "map this category" badge: ask the AI to link a category in the same setup. |
| Your other QuickBooks clients need a link | A new global rule files their transactions only once they link its global category. Until then, those transactions wait with the badge. Set up rules for those clients with the AI too, or link the category in the LedgerSync app. |

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="More messages: preview expired, setup not confirmed, LedgerSync busy, setup waiting, undo, setup failed, limits">

| The AI says | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| The preview expired | A preview works for 30 minutes. Ask the AI to preview again. |
| LedgerSync did not confirm the setup, so it may or may not have started | Don't ask it to apply again. Wait a minute, then ask it to check the setup's status. |
| LedgerSync is busy with other setups | Nothing was saved. Wait a minute, then ask the AI to apply the same preview again. A preview works for 30 minutes. |
| The setup is waiting | Your firm runs one setup at a time. It starts when the one before it finishes. |
| Too many items for one preview | One preview takes up to 100 rules, 50 new global categories, 50 new client categories, 100 category links and 100 new vendors. Ask the AI to do the rest in another preview. |
| A rule's words are refused | A rule needs 1 to 5 conditions on the bank text, each up to 200 characters. At least one must look for words (contains or is exactly). The AI says what is wrong. Ask it to fix the rule. |
| A name is too long | Names can be up to 64 characters for a global rule, a client category or a global vendor, 80 for a client vendor, and 100 for a client rule or a global category. A GL code can be up to 64. Give the AI a shorter one. |
| Undo is not possible | An undo works once a setup is no longer running, whether it is done, partly done or failed, and again after an undo that could not finish. It cannot start while another setup of your firm is running or being undone: ask the AI again when that one finishes. A setup that is undone cannot be undone again. Undoing global rules, global categories or global vendors needs **Manage Global Rules**. |
| The setup failed | What it saved before it stopped stays, marked LS-AI. To remove it, ask the AI to undo the setup. For help with why it failed, email <AiEmail /> and say which client. |
| The undo check expired | An undo check works for 15 minutes. Ask the AI to check the undo again. |
| Too many previews, setups or undos this hour or today | Each AI app you connect can preview 60 times an hour, save 30 setups a day and undo 10 a day. Try again next hour or tomorrow, as the AI says. |

    - The apply takes only the preview's `confirmation_id`. It works for 30 minutes, only for the person and the AI
      app that made the preview, and saves only the items the preview accepted. Calling the apply again with the
      same confirmation returns the first answer; it never applies twice.
    - The undo works the same way. The first call changes nothing: it returns what would be removed and kept, and
      an `undo_id`. Only a second call with that `undo_id`, within 15 minutes, undoes the setup. Both calls go to
      the same tool, marked as changing data, so Claude and ChatGPT ask before each one, the check included.
    - The preview, the apply and the undo need `write:accounting`; the other six tools need `read:accounting`. A
      read-only connection can read but cannot preview, apply or undo.
    - Rules setup can start without being asked. For a Manual or QuickBooks Online client, the first page of
      `ledgersync_list_transactions` also answers `transactions_without_rule`: how many of the client's transactions
      no rule and nobody filled yet, as in **Which transactions?** above, when there are any. Its `next_step` then
      tells the AI to offer rules and, after the user's yes, to call `ledgersync_get_rules_setup`.
      `ledgersync_list_transactions_to_categorize` offers the same after a push, when a transaction it lists has no
      rule. Neither offers on a read-only connection.
    - The instructions above are also an MCP prompt, `rules_setup` ("Set up rules for a client"), with optional
      `client` and `focus` arguments. Claude Code lists it as a command.
    - For a client on another accounting app, such as Zoho Books, `ledgersync_get_rules_setup` answers
      `supported: false`, and a preview is refused with `invalid_request`.
    - For a QuickBooks client whose QuickBooks connection is broken, the preview refuses the client's new categories,
      its new vendors and customer payees with `quickbooks_broken`, and the `next_step` says to reconnect QuickBooks.
      It refuses them with the same code when QuickBooks could not be read just now: the message then says to try
      again in a few minutes, and the `next_step` says to preview again then.
    - On the sandbox connector, an apply really saves, in the sandbox. For a QuickBooks client, it creates the new
      categories and vendors in the Intuit sandbox QuickBooks company the client is connected to.
    - See [For developers](/ai/developers) for every field.

  <Accordion title="For developers: limits">
    - One preview takes up to 100 rules, 50 new global categories, 50 new client categories, 100 category links
      and 100 new vendors (client and firm vendors together).
    - A rule has 1 to 5 conditions on the bank text (contains, doesn't contain or is exactly), each 1 to 200
      characters, and at least one of them contains or is exactly. There are no amount conditions.
    - Names: a client rule up to 100 characters, a global rule 64, a global category 100, a client category 64, a
      client vendor 80, a global vendor 64 and a GL code 64. Names are trimmed, and a name counts as taken whatever
      its capitals.
    - Each connected AI app can make 60 previews an hour, 30 applies a day and 10 undos a day. An apply counts only
      when it reaches LedgerSync.
    - LedgerSync takes a few applies at a time, shared by every firm. An apply past that is refused as busy, is not
      counted, and can be sent again a minute later with the same `confirmation_id`.
    - A firm runs one setup at a time. The next one waits until the one before it finishes. An undo does not wait:
      while another setup of the firm is running or being undone, it is refused with `invalid_request`, and can be
      sent again when that one finishes.
    - A preview's confirmation works for 30 minutes, an apply's answer is kept for 24 hours, and an undo check
      works for 15 minutes.
    - Each person sets up rules only for the clients they see in the LedgerSync app: an employee, the clients
      assigned to them. Global rules, global categories and global vendors also need **Manage Global Rules**.
